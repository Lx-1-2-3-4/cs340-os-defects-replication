# Figure 4d: what does not match and why

Figure 4d ("Overall" similarity) is the only place where the reproduction does
not match the paper. Everything else is exact: panels (a)–(c) 9/9, Table II
114/114, Tables III–V 438/438, Figures 2–3 39/39.

## The mismatch

| OS pair | Paper (Fig. 4d) | Ours (chosen rule) | Gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Android–Linux | 0.05 | 0.16 | +0.11 |
| Android–HarmonyOS | −0.11 | 0.08 | +0.19, sign flip |
| Linux–HarmonyOS | −0.04 | 0.05 | +0.09, sign flip |

"Ours" here is the rule described as D5 in [`DEVIATIONS.md`](DEVIATIONS.md):
concatenate the 21 categories of panels (a)–(c), drop categories that are zero for
both systems, compute Spearman's rho.

## The paper contradicts itself about this panel

The Figure 4d discussion says:

> "the highest absolute coefficient value is merely −0.11 (between Linux and
> Android)"

The heatmap, however, puts −0.11 in the Android–HarmonyOS cell; the
Android–Linux cell is 0.05. `data/paper/fig4.csv` follows the heatmap, since the
figure is what is being reproduced. This inconsistency is inside the paper and
does not depend on our recomputation.

## Checks we ran

| Check | Question | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recompute Table II from the same records | Are the input distributions right? | 114/114 match, so the vectors that go into Figure 4 are right. |
| Recompute panels (a)–(c) | Is the Spearman implementation right? | 9/9 coefficients match to two decimals. |
| Panel (c) without the zero-category rule | Is the discrepancy caused by preprocessing? | Without dropping categories that are zero in both systems, Android–HarmonyOS is 0.61 instead of the printed 0.38. With the rule it is 0.38, 0.21, 0.41, exactly the printed panel (c). The rule is therefore part of the paper's pipeline even though it is not stated. |
| Five candidate aggregation rules | Is the mismatch just a different but valid aggregation? | None reproduces the printed values. Table below. |
| Tie handling | Could tie-breaking explain it? | Mid-rank (average) ties reproduce all other panels. Min, max and dense variants do not produce panel (d) either. |

### Candidate rules for the "Overall" panel

Generated by `scripts/reproduce.py` into
[`results/fig4_overall_candidates.csv`](../results/fig4_overall_candidates.csv).

| Rule | Android–Linux | Android–HarmonyOS | Linux–HarmonyOS |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Paper (Figure 4d) | 0.05 | −0.11 | −0.04 |
| Concatenate 21 categories, drop shared zeros (our chosen rule) | 0.16 | 0.08 | 0.05 |
| Concatenate 21 categories, no filtering | 0.16 | 0.21 | 0.05 |
| Concatenate 26 categories (+ severity) | 0.13 | 0.14 | 0.03 |
| Concatenate 38 categories (+ severity + code elements) | 0.35 | 0.39 | 0.34 |
| Mean of the three marginal panels | 0.09 | 0.05 | −0.01 |

No rule produces the printed signs and magnitudes at the same time. The two
coincidences are single cells: `mean_of_marginal_panels` gives 0.05 for
Android–Linux, and `concat_21_drop_shared_zeros` gives 0.05 for Linux–HarmonyOS
where the paper prints −0.04.

## Three possible explanations

1. Unspecified aggregation. The paper never says how the panel was built.
   Different concatenation and weighting choices give coefficients between −0.01
   and 0.39 in our tests, so a different rule could in principle produce the
   printed numbers. None of the five we tried does.
2. A different snapshot of the data. The released dashboard may post-date the
   figure. Panels (a)–(c) would normally change too, and they do not, which makes
   this explanation less likely.
3. A write-up or figure error. The text/heatmap disagreement shows that at least
   one of the two descriptions of Figure 4d is wrong, so the printed matrix may
   be affected as well.

Panels (a)–(c) reproduce exactly, so the most defensible reading is that the
Figure 4d construction is under-specified and cannot be recovered from the
released data, and that the paper contradicts itself about this panel.

## What this means for Finding 5

The qualitative claim survives: "defect distributions differ substantially across
systems". The largest coefficient we computed is 0.39, so nothing here indicates
a strong positive relationship; this is consistent with the paper's −0.11 / 0.05 /
−0.04. That is why C3 is marked as partially supported in
[`CLAIMS.md`](CLAIMS.md).

The three printed panel-(d) numbers should be quoted as unreproduced until the
authors' aggregation rule is known. They cannot be used as evidence on their own.

## Limitation

We do not have the authors' analysis scripts or notes. A direct answer would need
the Figure 4d construction from them: which categories are included, whether
values are weighted per dimension, and which data snapshot was used. That is
outside the scope of this project.
